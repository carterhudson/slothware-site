Tripwire Privacy Policy

Effective 5 September 2026. Last updated 16 November 2026.

The short version. Tripwire has no accounts and no servers. Your places, rooms, and reminders are stored on your phone and are never sent to us — we have no way to see them. The app talks to Google only to draw maps, to look up a place you search for by name, and through Android's own speech, assistant, and backup features.

Who we are

Tripwire is made by Slothware. You can reach us at support@slothware.studio with any question about this policy or your data.

Everything below is stored in a database on your device. Slothware operates no servers and receives none of it.

What Tripwire does not collect

  • No account, sign-in, name, email address, or advertising identifier.
  • No audio recordings. Tripwire uses the microphone only while you are dictating a reminder, passes the audio straight to Android's speech recognizer, and keeps only the resulting text. Audio is never saved.
  • No analytics, advertising, tracking, or crash-reporting services. The app contains no such components.
  • No contacts, photos, files, or calendar data.

When the app talks to Google

Tripwire has no backend of its own. These features rely on Google services built into Android, or on your phone's assistant, and their handling is governed by Google's privacy policy or the assistant's own policy:

  • Maps and place search. When you pick a place, map imagery is fetched from Google Maps. When you type a place name, that text is sent to Google Places to return matching suggestions. Your own location is not attached to that search.
  • Dictation. While you speak a reminder, Tripwire streams the microphone audio to your phone's speech recognition service (usually Google's). That service may process it on the device or on Google's servers; Tripwire does not restrict it to on-device recognition. Tripwire receives only the transcribed text, which is then interpreted on-device by Android's built-in AI model. The phrase is not sent anywhere by the app.
  • Your phone's assistant. If your assistant supports Android app functions, you can ask it to create, find, or complete Tripwire reminders, or to pause and resume them. Tripwire answers those requests on your phone and sends nothing to us. What the assistant does with your request is governed by the assistant's own privacy policy.
  • Backup. If Android Backup is enabled on your phone, your Tripwire data is included in your device backup to your own Google Drive. On Android 9 and later, that backup is end-to-end encrypted with a key derived from your screen lock, so Google cannot read it. You can turn this off in your phone's system backup settings.

Why background location

A reminder that only worked while you had the app open would not be much of a reminder, so Tripwire asks for location access set to Allow all the time. Android will not deliver a place reminder without it. Your location is used solely to detect the boundary crossings you have set reminders for. It is not logged, profiled, or transmitted.

You can decline. The app still opens and you can still create reminders, but they will not fire, and Tripwire shows a standing warning while that is the case rather than letting you believe otherwise.

Keeping and deleting your data

Your data is kept until you delete it. Removing a reminder, room, or place deletes it from the device immediately. Uninstalling Tripwire removes the entire database.

If Android Backup is enabled, a copy may remain in your Google Drive backup after uninstalling. That copy is under your control, not ours, and can be removed through your Google account's backup settings.

Because we never receive your data, there is nothing for us to delete on request — and no breach of our systems could expose it.

Children

Tripwire is not directed to children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect information from them. As above, the app collects nothing to us regardless of who uses it.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the updated version will be posted here with a new date above. Material changes to what the app collects will also be described in the app's release notes.
